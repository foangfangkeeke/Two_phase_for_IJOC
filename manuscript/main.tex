\title{A Two-Phase Integer L-Shaped Method for Stochastic Electric-Bus Wireless Charging Infrastructure Planning}

\author[inst1]{Author Name}
\address[inst1]{Affiliation}

\begin{abstract}
Dynamic wireless charging (DWC) can reduce electric-bus battery requirements by supplying energy in transit.
DWC facilities are long-lived and must be located before future service plans are known.
This study examines how to coordinate DWC facility locations with scenario-dependent route-level battery configurations and representative-day charging schedules.
We formulate the problem as a two-stage stochastic program that minimizes facility, battery, and charging costs.
Directly applying the integer L-shaped method can produce a weak master approximation and require repeated solutions of large mixed-integer scenario problems whose routes share plug-in capacity.
To solve the problem, we develop a repair-enhanced two-phase integer L-shaped method (R-TPILS).
An equivalent reformulation lifts battery choices into the master and yields energy-budget valid inequalities.
Phase 1 relaxes the shared-capacity coupling and generates lower bounds and cuts from independent route-level linear programs.
In Phase 2, feasibility monotonicity and repair are used to recover feasible solutions and improve lower bounds.
Computational experiments on the Tower Transit bus network in Singapore compare R-TPILS with four other methods.
Within the time limit, only R-TPILS meets the 1\% gap target on all nine main instances.
Component analyses show that the inequalities screen infeasible configurations early and that Phase 1 rapidly establishes tight lower bounds.
On difficult instances, repair recovers verified feasible solutions and produces stronger monotone cuts, although its net benefit varies across instances.
In the planning comparison, allowing DWC reduces mean battery capacity by about 23\% and shifts energy supply from overnight to daytime charging.
\end{abstract}
