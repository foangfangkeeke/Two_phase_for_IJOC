\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies a two-stage stochastic DWC facility location problem for electric-bus networks under uncertain future service plans.
We identify two structural properties of its recourse: cross-route coupling arises only from shared plug-in capacity, and operational feasibility is monotone in DWC availability and battery capacity.
Building on these properties, we develop R-TPILS.
Its TPILS core combines an equivalent lifted reformulation, energy-budget inequalities, route LP relaxations, and exact scenario verification.
R-TPILS augments this core with a repair search and monotone feasibility cuts.

Computational results based on the Tower Transit bus network in Singapore support two main conclusions.
First, the components of R-TPILS address complementary computational bottlenecks.
The energy-budget inequalities screen infeasible configurations early, and Phase 1 quickly establishes tight lower bounds.
The repair search helps recover verified feasible solutions on difficult instances, and monotone cuts can further improve lower bounds.
These benefits vary across instances.
Overall, R-TPILS reaches the 1\% target on all nine main instances, compared with eight for TPILS and at most six for the remaining methods.
Second, DWC changes the balance between onboard storage and daytime energy supply.
Mean battery capacity decreases by about 23\%, while energy supply shifts from overnight toward daytime charging.

Several extensions appear promising.
First, accounting for battery weight in energy consumption would make the planning model more realistic by capturing the relationship between battery capacity and energy use.
Second, repair helps when feasible solutions to the exact scenario subproblems are hard to find, but its additional search can outweigh the benefit on easier instances.
Future work could investigate when and how repair should be applied.
Finally, similar capacity coupling and feasibility monotonicity may arise in other transport planning problems, including berth planning, bus depot planning, and electric-truck charging infrastructure planning.
The proposed framework could therefore be extended to these settings.
