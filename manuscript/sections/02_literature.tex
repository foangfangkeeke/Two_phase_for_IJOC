\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature}

This paper intersects two literature streams.
The first studies charging infrastructure, battery configuration, and energy-feasible operations for electric buses; the second develops exact decomposition methods for stochastic design problems with mixed-integer recourse.

\subsection{Electric-bus charging and wireless infrastructure planning}
\label{subsec:lit-wireless-bus}

% TODO: Cite integrated electric-bus infrastructure, battery-sizing, and scheduling studies.
Electric-bus planning links strategic infrastructure decisions with tactical battery and charging decisions.
Charging-site selection and battery capacity determine the resources available to a fleet, while vehicle assignment, charging schedules, and route energy constraints determine whether those resources can support the prescribed service.
Integrated optimization models therefore evaluate infrastructure and battery investments together with energy-feasible service operations.

% TODO: Cite depot, terminal, plug-in, and opportunity-charging studies with limited charger capacity.
Depot and terminal charging studies focus on the timing of plug-in charging under vehicle schedules, electricity prices, and charger-capacity limits.
Opportunity-charging models extend this interaction to charging during service, allowing energy to be replenished between trips or at selected stops.
When chargers have finite capacity, station--time occupancy constraints couple buses or routes that would otherwise admit separate energy-feasibility calculations.

% TODO: Cite SWC/DWC planning studies and work jointly considering wireless facilities and battery capacity.
Wireless charging introduces a further infrastructure layer.
SWC creates charging opportunities at selected stations, whereas DWC supplies energy while a bus traverses an equipped road segment.
The associated planning literature examines how the location and extent of these facilities affect battery requirements, route energy feasibility, and investment and charging costs.
These models establish the facility--battery trade-off that underlies the present application.

Together, these studies provide the main application components of our model: wireless-facility placement, route-level battery configuration, energy-feasible trip chains, and limited plug-in charging resources.
We combine these components under service-plan uncertainty, with wireless facilities selected before the future service plan is known and battery configurations and charging decisions adapted to its realization.
This setting links long-term infrastructure planning to shared-resource mixed-integer recourse.

\subsection{Decomposition methods for stochastic design problems}
\label{subsec:lit-stochastic-decomposition}

% TODO: Cite stochastic facility-location and network-design models with scenario recourse.
Two-stage stochastic models for facility location and network design separate infrastructure choices made before uncertainty from scenario-dependent recourse.
Scenario decomposition is particularly attractive when the deterministic equivalent is large, because fixing the first-stage design separates the evaluation of distinct scenarios.
The computational challenge increases when the recourse contains discrete configuration or operating decisions rather than a continuous linear program.

% TODO: Cite foundational Benders, integer L-shaped, and logic-based Benders methods.
Benders decomposition and the L-shaped method use subproblem information to iteratively refine a master representation of future cost and feasibility.
For continuous recourse, linear-programming duality supplies this information directly.
Mixed-integer recourse instead requires integer L-shaped, logic-based, or related combinatorial mechanisms, and exact scenario evaluation can become the dominant computational burden.

% TODO: Cite enriched-master, partial-decomposition, recourse-relaxation, and nested-decomposition studies.
One line of research improves this process by changing the decomposition architecture.
Richer master formulations expose selected recourse decisions or partial recourse descriptions, while lower-bounding relaxations and internal subproblem decompositions reduce the cost of scenario evaluation.
These choices determine both the strength of the master bound and the computational effort required to generate it.

% TODO: Cite combinatorial, dominance-based, and monotonicity-based Benders cuts.
A complementary line of research strengthens the information returned by the subproblems.
Logic-based and combinatorial cuts exploit discrete feasibility structure, while dominance relations and problem-specific valid inequalities can extend a correction beyond the single configuration at which it was generated.
Such strengthening depends on a verifiable ordering or implication structure in the underlying recourse model.

% TODO: Reassess the methodological gap after the final decomposition reference set is assembled.
These studies show that master representation, subproblem organization, and cut design must be matched to the recourse structure.
The setting considered here combines scenario-dependent discrete battery configurations, conditionally route-local operations, and limited shared-resource coupling within each scenario.
This layered structure motivates an exact decomposition method designed around their interaction rather than a direct application of scenario decomposition alone.
