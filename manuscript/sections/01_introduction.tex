\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Electric buses are being adopted to reduce local air pollution and noise, but their limited driving range may require recharging during the day \citep{devos_capacitated_2024}.
Dynamic wireless charging (DWC) can supplement plug-in charging by supplying energy to vehicles while they are moving.
DWC is particularly well suited to bus systems, where fixed routes and frequent services allow the same charging infrastructure to serve many buses repeatedly.

DWC deployment involves substantial upfront investment in infrastructure intended to remain in use for many years.
During this period, changes in passenger demand may require adjustments to bus service plans \citep{perumal_review_2022}.
The deployment decision must therefore account for how the facilities will be used under uncertain future service plans.
We study this decision in a bus network with given plug-in charger locations and capacities.
The DWC layout is chosen before the service plan is known.
Once a service-plan scenario is realized, the planner selects a route-level battery configuration for that planning period and a representative-day charging schedule.
Because routes share the existing plug-in stations, their charging schedules must jointly satisfy station capacity limits.
The resulting two-stage stochastic DWC planning model is a mixed-integer program.
With all costs expressed on a representative-day basis, the model minimizes DWC facility cost and expected battery and charging costs.

The two-stage structure suggests an integer L-shaped (ILS) decomposition, with DWC decisions in the master and battery and charging decisions in scenario subproblems.
A direct application faces two computational difficulties.
The master represents the feasibility and recourse cost of a DWC layout through cuts.
A weak approximation can lead to repeated evaluations of layouts whose infeasibility or high recourse cost is revealed only after the scenario subproblems are solved.
At the same time, each scenario subproblem remains a large mixed-integer linear program (MILP) after scenario decomposition.
Battery and charger-occupancy decisions are discrete, while shared plug-in charging capacity couples the routes within the subproblem.
Repeated exact solution of integer recourse can be expensive \citep{larsen_learning_2024}, magnifying the cost of a weak master approximation.
These difficulties motivate a method that strengthens the master's representation of recourse and obtains useful bounds before exact scenario evaluation.

Our contributions are threefold.
\begin{enumerate}
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\roman{enumi}.}
\item We formulate a two-stage stochastic DWC planning model that coordinates long-term DWC deployment with scenario-dependent route battery configurations and representative-day charging schedules.
We characterize its recourse structure, in which shared capacity at the existing plug-in stations is the sole source of coupling between routes.
\item To solve the model, we derive an equivalent reformulation and use it to develop a two-phase integer L-shaped method (TPILS) that exploits the route separability revealed when shared plug-in capacity constraints are relaxed.
We further develop a repair-enhanced variant (R-TPILS) that uses feasibility monotonicity to strengthen the decomposition.
\item We conduct numerical experiments based on a 28-route Tower Transit network in Singapore to evaluate the proposed methods using the extensive formulation (EF) and ILS as baselines.
A planning case study compares DWC-enabled and no-DWC plans in terms of battery capacity and charging allocation.
\end{enumerate}
