\section{Problem Setting and Formulation}
\label{sec:model}

This section defines the directed station network and its scheduled traversal events and formulates the DWC facility location problem as a two-stage stochastic program.

\subsection{Problem description}
\label{subsec:network-scenarios}
Consider an electric bus system with a set of stations $\mathcal S$ and a set of directed links $\mathcal L$.
A link connects consecutive stations along a bus duty, including links traversed between consecutive trips.
Existing plug-in chargers are available at stations in $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal S$.
These chargers have given capacities and are shared by buses in the system.
DWC facilities may be installed on candidate links in $\mathcal L^{\mathrm D}\subseteq\mathcal L$.

The system operates a set of routes $\mathcal R$.
For a given service plan, each bus duty consists of an ordered sequence of trips assigned to one bus and any travel between consecutive trips.
A bus departs from the depot with its battery at the upper state-of-charge limit and then operates its assigned trips in sequence.
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:daily-duty-operation}, during its duty the bus may use plug-in charging and receive DWC while traversing equipped links.
After completing its last trip, the bus returns to the depot and is recharged overnight to the upper state-of-charge limit before the next operating day.
Depot charging capacity is sufficient to recharge all buses overnight.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/duty_chain.pdf}
    \caption{Illustration of a bus duty, its charging opportunities, and a plug-in station shared with other buses.}
    \label{fig:daily-duty-operation}
\end{figure}

We consider alternative service plans for a single planning period.
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:two-stage-decisions}, the planner first selects the links on which DWC facilities are installed, before the service plan is known.
For each possible service plan, the planner then chooses a matching route-level battery configuration and representative-day charging plan under the same facility layout.
The service plan and battery configuration remain fixed throughout the planning period.

\subsection{Two-stage stochastic formulation}
\label{subsec:two-stage-formulation}

\subsubsection{Overall formulation and first-stage decisions}
\label{subsubsec:first-stage}

The possible service plans are represented by scenarios $\omega\in\Omega$, each with probability $p_\omega>0$, where $\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega=1$.
All costs are expressed on a representative-day basis, and the notation is summarized in \ref{app:model-details}.
$C_\omega(u_\omega)$ represents the second-stage cost for scenario $\omega$.
The binary variable $w_l$ indicates whether DWC is installed on candidate link $l$, and $u_\omega$ represents the battery and charging decisions for scenario $\omega$.
The model minimizes the DWC facility cost and the expected battery and charging costs:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \min_{w,\{u_\omega\}_{\omega\in\Omega}}\quad
    & C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)
    + \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega C_\omega(u_\omega) \\
    \mathrm{s.t.}\quad
    & w\in\mathcal W, \\
    & u_\omega\in\mathcal U_\omega(w), \qquad \omega\in\Omega.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:two-stage-model}
\end{equation}
In \eqref{eq:two-stage-model}, $\mathcal W$ is the first-stage feasible set, and $\mathcal U_\omega(w)$ is the second-stage feasible set under scenario $\omega$ and facility layout $w$.
The first-stage feasible set and facility cost are
\begin{align}
    \mathcal W &= \{0,1\}^{|\mathcal L^{\mathrm D}|},
    \label{eq:dwc-domain} \\
    C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w) &= \sum_{l\in\mathcal L^{\mathrm D}} c_l^{\mathrm D}w_l, \notag
\end{align}
where $c_l^{\mathrm D}$ is the cost of installing DWC on link $l$.

\subsubsection{Second-stage decisions and constraints}
\label{subsubsec:second-stage}

For scenario $\omega$, write $u_\omega=(z_\omega,x_\omega,y_\omega)$, where $z_\omega$ collects route-level battery selections, $x_\omega$ plug-in charger occupancy decisions, and $y_\omega$ charging amounts and battery energy states.
The scenario cost is $C_\omega(u_\omega)=C^{\mathrm{bat}}_\omega(z_\omega)+C^{\mathrm{chg}}_\omega(y_\omega)$.
The following constraints define $\mathcal U_\omega(w)$ for each scenario $\omega\in\Omega$.

\paragraph{Battery configuration and cost}
Battery option $k\in\mathcal K$ has capacity $\beta_k$, and the scenario-dependent binary variable $z_{\omega,r,k}$ indicates whether it is selected for route $r$.
The route-level battery-choice constraints are
\begin{align}
    & \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} z_{\omega,r,k} = 1,
    && r \in \mathcal{R},
    \label{eq:one-battery} \\
    & B_{\omega,r}
    =
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \beta_k z_{\omega,r,k},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},
    \label{eq:battery-size} \\
    & z_{\omega,r,k} \in \{0,1\},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ k \in \mathcal{K}.
    \label{eq:z-domain}
\end{align}
Constraints~\eqref{eq:one-battery}--\eqref{eq:z-domain} select one common battery option for all buses assigned to each route and define the induced route battery capacity $B_{\omega,r}$.
For the set $\mathcal M_{\omega,r}$ of bus duties on route $r$, $|\mathcal M_{\omega,r}|$ is the number of buses.
Let $c^{\mathrm{bat}}$ denote the battery capacity cost per kWh.
The scenario battery cost is
\[
    C^{\mathrm{bat}}_\omega(z_\omega)
    =
    c^{\mathrm{bat}}
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    |\mathcal M_{\omega,r}|
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}}
    \beta_k z_{\omega,r,k}.
\]
This term gives the battery capacity cost for all buses required under the service plan; the acquisition or disposal of vehicles and batteries associated with changes in fleet size is not modeled separately.

Across the service plans, $\mathcal V$ and $\mathcal A$ denote the sets of station-visit and link-traversal events generated by bus duties, respectively.
Each duty is represented by an ordered sequence of station visits as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:daily-duty-operation}.
Each visit $v\in\mathcal V$ is a distinct occurrence in a duty, and $s(v)$ is its physical station.
Each duty, visit, and traversal belongs to a single scenario--route pair.
Each traversal $a=(v,v')\in\mathcal A$ connects consecutive visits on the same duty and is associated with the physical link $\ell(a)=(s(v),s(v'))$.
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:link-event-mapping}, the same physical link may be traversed multiple times by the same bus or by different buses, with each traversal represented by a distinct event.

\paragraph{Shared plug-in capacity}
The station visits on route $r$ in scenario $\omega$ are denoted by $\mathcal V_{\omega,r}$, and $\mathcal V^{\mathrm P}_{\omega,r}$ contains the terminal visits eligible for plug-in charging under the service plan.
An eligible visit $v$ can charge during its available time slots $\mathcal T_v\subseteq\mathcal T_\omega$, where $\mathcal T_\omega$ contains the relevant time slots in scenario $\omega$.
The variable $q_v^{\mathrm P}$ denotes the grid-side energy supplied and $x_{v\tau}$ indicates whether a visit occupies a charger in slot $\tau$.
The plug-in charging constraints are
\begin{align}
    & \left\{\begin{aligned}
        0 \leq q_v^{\mathrm P}
        &\leq \bar q^{\mathrm P}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T_v}x_{v\tau},
        && r\in\mathcal R,\ v\in\mathcal V^{\mathrm P}_{\omega,r}, \\
        q_v^{\mathrm P} &= 0,
        && r\in\mathcal R,\ v\in\mathcal V_{\omega,r}\setminus\mathcal V^{\mathrm P}_{\omega,r},
    \end{aligned}\right.
    \label{eq:plugin-charge-limit} \\
    & \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{\substack{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}:\\ s(v)=c,\ \tau\in\mathcal{T}_v}}
    x_{v\tau}
    \leq
    N_c,
    && c \in \mathcal{C},\ \tau \in \mathcal{T}_\omega,
    \label{eq:plugin-capacity} \\
    & x_{v\tau} \in \{0,1\},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r},\ \tau \in \mathcal{T}_v.
    \label{eq:x-domain}
\end{align}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:plugin-charge-limit} limits grid-side energy delivery to $\bar q^{\mathrm P}$ per occupied slot and excludes charging at ineligible visits.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:plugin-capacity} limits simultaneous charger occupancy at station $c$ to the number $N_c$ of existing chargers and couples buses using the same station.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:x-domain} specifies binary occupancy decisions.

\paragraph{Other operating constraints and charging cost}
Let $\mathcal A_{\omega,r}$ denote the link traversals on route $r$ in scenario $\omega$, and $\mathcal A^{\mathrm D}_{\omega,r}$ the subset on candidate DWC links.
For a traversal $a$, $q_a^{\mathrm D}$ denotes the grid-side DWC energy supplied; for a duty $m\in\mathcal M_{\omega,r}$, $q_m^{\mathrm N}$ denotes the grid-side overnight energy supplied.
The DWC energy bound $\bar q_a^{\mathrm D}$ is the maximum amount of energy that can be supplied via DWC for a given traversal.
The battery energy levels upon arrival at and departure from visit $v$ are denoted by $e_v^{\mathrm{arr}}$ and $e_v^{\mathrm{dep}}$, respectively.
For duty $m$, $v_m^0$ and $v_m^N$ denote its first and last station visits, and $d_m^0$ and $d_m^N$ denote the energy consumed traveling from and to the depot, respectively.
The consumption $d_a$ is assumed to be known and fixed for each traversal $a$, and $\eta^{\mathrm P}$ and $\eta^{\mathrm D}$ convert grid-side charging energy to battery energy.
The charging and energy variables in $y_\omega$ are nonnegative.
For each route $r\in\mathcal R$, the operating constraints are
\begingroup
\allowdisplaybreaks[2]
\begin{align}
    & \left\{\begin{aligned}
        0 \leq q_a^{\mathrm D}
        &\leq \bar q_a^{\mathrm D}w_{\ell(a)},
        && a\in\mathcal A^{\mathrm D}_{\omega,r}, \\
        q_a^{\mathrm D} &= 0,
        && a\in\mathcal A_{\omega,r}\setminus\mathcal A^{\mathrm D}_{\omega,r},
    \end{aligned}\right.
    \label{eq:dwc-charge-limit} \\
    & \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    \leq
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    \leq
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:soc-arr-bound} \\
    & \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    \leq
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    \leq
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:soc-dep-bound} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    =
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{P}}
    q_v^{\mathrm{P}},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:station-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{arr}}_{v'}
    =
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    -
    d_a
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{D}}
    q_a^{\mathrm{D}},
    && a=(v,v') \in \mathcal{A}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:link-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{arr}}_{v_m^{0}}
    =
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    -
    d_m^{0},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:initial-boundary-energy} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{v_m^{N}}
    -
    d_m^{N}
    \geq
    \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:return-boundary-energy} \\
    & \eta^{\mathrm{P}} q_m^{\mathrm{N}}
    =
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    -
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{v_m^{N}}
    +
    d_m^{N},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}.
    \label{eq:overnight-charge-balance}
\end{align}
\endgroup
Constraint~\eqref{eq:dwc-charge-limit} links DWC charging to facility decisions, and constraints~\eqref{eq:soc-arr-bound}--\eqref{eq:soc-dep-bound} impose the state-of-charge limits $\underline\rho$ and $\overline\rho$.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:station-energy-balance}--\eqref{eq:overnight-charge-balance} describe energy balances and depot operations for each duty.

At daytime and overnight electricity prices $c^{\mathrm{day}}\geq 0$ and $c^{\mathrm{night}}\geq 0$, the scenario charging cost is
\[
    C^{\mathrm{chg}}_\omega(y_\omega)
    =
    c^{\mathrm{day}}
    \left(
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}} q_v^{\mathrm{P}}
    +
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}^{\mathrm D}_{\omega,r}} q_a^{\mathrm{D}}
    \right)
    +
    c^{\mathrm{night}}
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}}
    q_m^{\mathrm{N}}.
\]
