\section{Problem Formulation and Baseline Decomposition}
\label{sec:model}

This section formulates the wireless charging infrastructure planning problem as a two-stage stochastic program.
The formulation separates long-lived facility decisions from scenario-adaptive battery and charging decisions, which provides the baseline decomposition used in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Two-stage stochastic formulation}
\label{subsec:two-stage-formulation}

Let $\Omega$ be the set of service-plan scenarios, and let $p_\omega$ be the probability of scenario $\omega \in \Omega$, where $p_\omega\geq 0$ and $\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega=1$.
Each scenario represents one plausible future service plan and fixes the route frequencies, timetables, bus trip chains, active-fleet requirements, and trip-level energy requirements, while the operated route set $\mathcal{R}$ is common across scenarios.
The planner first installs long-lived wireless charging facilities before the future service plan is known.
After a scenario is realized, the operator specifies route-level battery capacities and charging-allocation decisions for the resulting trip chains.

Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the station set, $\mathcal{L}$ the directed link set between consecutive stations, and $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}$ the set of existing interchanges or hubs equipped with plug-in chargers.
Stationary wireless charging (SWC) can be installed at any station in $\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C}$, while dynamic wireless charging (DWC) can be installed on any directed link in $\mathcal{L}$.
The first-stage binary variables $w_s^{\mathrm{S}}$ and $w_l^{\mathrm{D}}$ indicate whether SWC is installed at station $s\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C}$ and DWC is installed on link $l\in\mathcal{L}$, respectively.
Existing plug-in chargers at stations in $\mathcal{C}$ are pre-existing resources with limited shared capacity and are not candidates for SWC deployment.

The compact two-stage stochastic formulation is
\begin{align}
    \min_{w^{\mathrm{S}},w^{\mathrm{D}}} \quad
        & C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)
        +
        \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} p_\omega \Phi_\omega(w)
        \label{eq:two-stage-model} \\
    \mathrm{s.t.} \quad
        & w_s^{\mathrm{S}} \in \{0,1\},
        && s \in \mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C},
        \label{eq:swc-domain} \\
        & w_l^{\mathrm{D}} \in \{0,1\},
        && l \in \mathcal{L}.
        \label{eq:dwc-domain}
\end{align}
where $w=(w^{\mathrm{S}},w^{\mathrm{D}})$ and
\[
    C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)=\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C}} c_s^{\mathrm{S}} w_s^{\mathrm{S}}+\sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}} c_l^{\mathrm{D}} w_l^{\mathrm{D}}.
\]
All cost coefficients are expressed on a common planning-period equivalent basis.
The vector $w$ is the nonanticipative here-and-now facility decision, and $\Phi_\omega(w)$ is the optimal second-stage value in scenario $\omega$.

The scenario decisions consist of route-level battery selection variables $z_{\omega,r,k}$ and operating variables collected in $y_\omega$, including charging amounts, plug-in occupancy, and battery energy states.
Bus fleet size and battery residual values are treated as fixed scenario data, as they are known once a scenario is realized.
For a fixed facility layout $w$, the scenario recourse value $\Phi_\omega(w)$ is the minimum scenario cost
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_\omega(w)
    =
    \min_{z_\omega,y_\omega}
    \left\{
        C^{\mathrm{bat}}_\omega(z_\omega) + C^{\mathrm{chg}}_\omega(y_\omega):
        (z_\omega,y_\omega)\in\mathcal{X}_\omega(w)
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:recourse-value}
\end{equation}
For each scenario $\omega$, the recourse formulation is defined on route-indexed trip-chain data.
For route $r\in\mathcal R$, let $\mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}$ denote its trip chains and $\mathcal{A}_{\omega,r}$ its directed traversals.
Each trip chain corresponds to one bus; let $n_{\omega,r}=|\mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}|$ denote the route fleet size under scenario $\omega$.
Let $\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}$ and $\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega,r}$ denote plug-in and SWC station visits.
Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the set of battery options, and let $\beta_k$ be the capacity of option $k$.
The scenario battery and charging costs are
\[
\begin{aligned}
    C^{\mathrm{bat}}_\omega(z_\omega)
    &=
    c^{\mathrm{bat}}
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    n_{\omega,r}
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}}
    \beta_k z_{\omega,r,k}, \\
    C^{\mathrm{chg}}_\omega(y_\omega)
    &=
    c^{\mathrm{day}}
    \left(
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}} q_v^{\mathrm{P}}
    +
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega,r}} q_v^{\mathrm{S}}
    +
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_{\omega,r}} q_a^{\mathrm{D}}
    \right)
    +
    c^{\mathrm{night}}
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}}
    q_m^{\mathrm{N}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The feasible set $\mathcal{X}_\omega(w)$ is described by the following constraints.
The battery-choice constraints are
\begin{align}
    & \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} z_{\omega,r,k} = 1,
    && r \in \mathcal{R},
    \label{eq:one-battery} \\
    & B_{\omega,r}
    =
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \beta_k z_{\omega,r,k},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},
    \label{eq:battery-size} \\
    & z_{\omega,r,k} \in \{0,1\},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ k \in \mathcal{K}.
    \label{eq:z-domain}
\end{align}
Let $\mathcal{T}_\omega$ be the plug-in time-slot set in scenario $\omega$, let $\mathcal{T}_v\subseteq\mathcal{T}_\omega$ denote the plug-in time slots available to visit $v$, and let $s(v)$ map visit $v$ to its station.
Let $\kappa$ be the grid-side energy delivered by one occupied plug-in time slot, and let $U_s$ be the plug-in charger capacity at station $s$.
The plug-in charging constraints are
\begin{align}
    & 0
    \leq
    q_v^{\mathrm{P}}
    \leq
    \kappa
    \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_v} x_{v\tau},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:plugin-charge-limit} \\
    & \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{\substack{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}:\\ s(v)=s,\ \tau\in\mathcal{T}_v}}
    x_{v\tau}
    \leq
    U_s,
    && s \in \mathcal{C},\ \tau \in \mathcal{T}_\omega,
    \label{eq:plugin-capacity} \\
    & x_{v\tau} \in \{0,1\},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r},\ \tau \in \mathcal{T}_v.
    \label{eq:x-domain}
\end{align}
Let $\mathcal{V}_{\omega,r}$ denote the station visits of route $r$ under scenario $\omega$, with plug-in and SWC visits forming its two visit types.
The mappings $\ell(a)$, $\mathrm{tail}(a)$, and $\mathrm{head}(a)$ map a directed traversal to its physical link and adjacent visits.
Here $\underline{\rho}$ and $\overline{\rho}$ are admissible State of Charge; $v_m^0$, $v_m^N$, $d_m^0$, $d_m^N$, and $q_m^{\mathrm{N}}$ describe the first and last visits, depot movements, and overnight depot charging for trip chain $m$.
Trip-level energy requirements are exogenous and do not vary with the selected battery capacity.
Each visit and traversal is a scenario-route specific event copy, so the corresponding scenario and route indices are suppressed from event-level variables.
All charging quantities are nonnegative continuous variables measured on the grid side, and all battery-energy states are continuous.
For each route $r\in\mathcal R$, the route energy constraints are
\begin{align}
    & \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    \leq
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    \leq
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:soc-arr-bound} \\
    & \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    \leq
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    \leq
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:soc-dep-bound} \\
    & 0
    \leq
    q_v^{\mathrm{S}}
    \leq
    \bar{q}^{\mathrm{S}}_v w_{s(v)}^{\mathrm{S}},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:swc-charge-limit} \\
    & 0
    \leq
    q_a^{\mathrm{D}}
    \leq
    \bar{q}^{\mathrm{D}}_a w_{\ell(a)}^{\mathrm{D}},
    && a \in \mathcal{A}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:dwc-charge-limit} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    =
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{P}}
    q_v^{\mathrm{P}},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:station-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    =
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{S}}
    q_v^{\mathrm{S}},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:swc-station-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{arr}}_{\mathrm{head}(a)}
    =
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{\mathrm{tail}(a)}
    -
    d_a
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{D}}
    q_a^{\mathrm{D}},
    && a \in \mathcal{A}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:link-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{arr}}_{v_m^{0}}
    =
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    -
    d_m^{0},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:initial-boundary-energy} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{v_m^{N}}
    -
    d_m^{N}
    \geq
    \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:return-boundary-energy} \\
    & \eta^{\mathrm{P}} q_m^{\mathrm{N}}
    =
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    -
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{v_m^{N}}
    +
    d_m^{N},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}.
    \label{eq:overnight-charge-balance}
\end{align}
Constraints~\eqref{eq:one-battery}--\eqref{eq:z-domain} define the route-level battery choice and the induced battery capacity.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:plugin-charge-limit}--\eqref{eq:x-domain} define plug-in charging amounts, charger occupancy, and station-time capacity.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:soc-arr-bound}--\eqref{eq:soc-dep-bound} impose arrival and departure SoC bounds, and \eqref{eq:swc-charge-limit}--\eqref{eq:dwc-charge-limit} define SWC and DWC charging amounts enabled by installed wireless facilities.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:station-energy-balance}--\eqref{eq:link-energy-balance} update energy at plug-in visits, SWC visits, and directed traversals, while \eqref{eq:initial-boundary-energy}--\eqref{eq:overnight-charge-balance} close each trip chain through depot movements and overnight recharge.

\subsection{Natural integer L-shaped benchmark}
\label{subsec:baseline-ils}

The two-stage stochastic formulation admits a standard integer L-shaped decomposition.
The master problem retains the first-stage facility vector $w=(w^{\mathrm S},w^{\mathrm D})$ and introduces $\theta_\omega$ to approximate the recourse value of each scenario.
For a trial facility layout $\bar w$, each scenario defines one mixed-integer subproblem consisting of the recourse objective \eqref{eq:recourse-value} and constraints \eqref{eq:one-battery}--\eqref{eq:overnight-charge-balance}.
Standard integer L-shaped feasibility and optimality cuts are generated from these scenario subproblems and added iteratively to the master problem.
We refer to this direct implementation as the natural integer L-shaped (Natural ILS) benchmark.
It leaves route-level battery selection, plug-in occupancy integrality, and shared plug-in charger capacity inside each scenario subproblem.
