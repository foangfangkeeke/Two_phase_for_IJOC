\section{Problem Formulation and Natural Decomposition}
\label{sec:model}

This section defines the stochastic wireless-charging infrastructure planning problem and the natural decomposition induced by its two-stage structure.
The model is written at the level needed for the algorithmic developments in Section~\ref{sec:method}; detailed opportunity-window constraints can be expanded in an appendix if needed.

\subsection{Problem setting and two-stage decision structure}
\label{subsec:problem-setting}

We study wireless charging infrastructure planning under a finite set of medium-term service-plan scenarios, rather than single-day operational dispatch.
Each scenario represents a possible future service plan and induces a representative operating day with route frequencies, timetables, bus trip chains, fleet requirements, and energy requirements.

The first-stage decision installs stationary wireless charging (SWC) facilities and dynamic wireless charging (DWC) links before the service-plan scenario is realized.
After a scenario is realized, the operator adapts route-level battery capacities and charging operations to that scenario.
Existing plug-in and fast-charging facilities are treated as exogenous operational resources, so the strategic facility-location decision concerns only new wireless charging infrastructure.

\subsection{Two-stage extensive-form formulation}
\label{subsec:two-stage-formulation}

Let $\Omega$ be the set of service-plan scenarios, and let $p_\omega$ be the probability of scenario $\omega \in \Omega$.
Let $\mathcal{J}$ be the set of candidate wireless charging facilities, with $\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{SWC}}$ denoting candidate stationary charging locations and $\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{DWC}}$ denoting candidate dynamic charging links.
The first-stage binary variable $w_j$ equals one if facility $j \in \mathcal{J}$ is installed.
The daily-equivalent facility cost is
\begin{equation}
    C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w) =
    \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}} c^{\mathrm{fac}}_j w_j.
    \label{eq:facility-cost}
\end{equation}

For each scenario $\omega$, the recourse decision includes a route-level battery configuration $z_\omega$ and an operating decision vector $y_\omega$.
The binary variable $z_{\omega r k}$ equals one if route $r \in \mathcal{R}_\omega$ uses battery option $k \in \mathcal{K}_r$, and $y_\omega$ collects charging, plug-in occupancy, and energy-state decisions on the representative operating day.
Table~\ref{tab:notation} summarizes the notation used in the formulation.

For a fixed facility layout $w$, the scenario recourse value is
\begin{align}
    \Phi_\omega(w)
    =
    \min_{z_\omega,y_\omega} \quad
        & C^{\mathrm{bat}}_\omega(z_\omega) + C^{\mathrm{op}}_\omega(y_\omega)
        \label{eq:recourse-value} \\
    \mathrm{s.t.} \quad
        & \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_r} z_{\omega r k} = 1,
        && r \in \mathcal{R}_\omega,
        \label{eq:one-battery} \\
        & B_{\omega r}
        =
        \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_r} \beta_{rk} z_{\omega r k},
        && r \in \mathcal{R}_\omega,
        \label{eq:battery-size} \\
        & \underline{\rho} B_{\omega r(e)}
        \leq
        E_{\omega e t}
        \leq
        \overline{\rho} B_{\omega r(e)},
        && e \in \mathcal{E}_\omega,\ t \in \mathcal{T}_{\omega e},
        \label{eq:soc-bound} \\
        & E_{\omega e,t+1}
        =
        E_{\omega e t}
        -
        d_{\omega e t}
        +
        \eta q_{\omega e t},
        && e \in \mathcal{E}_\omega,\ t \in \mathcal{T}_{\omega e},
        \label{eq:energy-balance} \\
        & q^{\mathrm{wc}}_{\omega e t j}
        \leq
        M w_j,
        && e \in \mathcal{E}_\omega,\ t \in \mathcal{T}_{\omega e},\ j \in \mathcal{J},
        \label{eq:facility-availability} \\
        & \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_\omega} x_{\omega e \tau s}
        \leq
        a_s,
        && s \in \mathcal{P},\ \tau \in \mathcal{H}_\omega,
        \label{eq:plugin-capacity} \\
        & y_\omega \in \mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}_\omega(w,z_\omega),
        \label{eq:operation-feasible-set} \\
        & z_{\omega r k} \in \{0,1\},
        && r \in \mathcal{R}_\omega,\ k \in \mathcal{K}_r.
        \label{eq:z-domain}
\end{align}
The recourse model selects one battery-capacity option for each route and enforces energy feasibility along each bus trip chain.
Wireless charging variables are linked to the first-stage facility layout, whereas plug-in charging is limited by the capacity of existing chargers.
The compact set $\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}_\omega(w,z_\omega)$ collects charging-window, duration, and variable-domain details.

The two-stage stochastic formulation is
\begin{align}
    \min_{w} \quad
        & C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)
        +
        \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} p_\omega \Phi_\omega(w)
        \label{eq:two-stage-model} \\
    \mathrm{s.t.} \quad
        & w_j \in \{0,1\},
        && j \in \mathcal{J}.
        \label{eq:w-domain}
\end{align}
The vector $w$ is nonanticipative across scenarios, while $z_\omega$ and $y_\omega$ are scenario-adaptive recourse decisions.
Equations~\eqref{eq:recourse-value}--\eqref{eq:w-domain} define the deterministic equivalent when the scenario recourse constraints are written explicitly for all scenarios.
Direct solution of this extensive form is used only later as a computational benchmark.

\subsection{Natural integer L-shaped baseline}
\label{subsec:natural-ils}

Because scenarios are coupled only through the first-stage facility vector $w$, the formulation in \eqref{eq:two-stage-model} directly induces a natural integer L-shaped decomposition.
The baseline master problem keeps $w$ and a recourse estimator $\theta_\omega$ for each scenario:
\begin{align}
    \min_{w,\theta} \quad
        & C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)
        +
        \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} p_\omega \theta_\omega
        \label{eq:natural-master} \\
    \mathrm{s.t.} \quad
        & \theta_\omega
        \geq
        \alpha_{\omega c}
        +
        \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \pi_{\omega c j} w_j,
        && \omega \in \Omega,\ c \in \mathcal{C}_\omega,
        \label{eq:natural-cuts} \\
        & w_j \in \{0,1\},
        && j \in \mathcal{J}.
        \label{eq:natural-master-domain}
\end{align}

Given a master solution $\bar{w}$, the scenario subproblem evaluates $\Phi_\omega(\bar{w})$ by solving \eqref{eq:recourse-value}--\eqref{eq:z-domain} independently for each scenario.
If the estimator $\theta_\omega$ is too low or if the recourse problem is infeasible, the algorithm adds an integer L-shaped cut to $\mathcal{C}_\omega$ and resolves the master.

This natural decomposition is a classical baseline rather than a contribution of this paper.
When the cut loop converges, it solves the two-stage formulation in \eqref{eq:two-stage-model}.
Section~\ref{sec:method} improves this baseline by changing the master representation, recourse evaluation, and cut construction.
