\section{Problem Formulation and Recourse Structure}
\label{sec:model}

This section formulates the wireless charging infrastructure planning problem as a two-stage stochastic program.
The formulation separates long-lived wireless-facility decisions from scenario-adaptive battery and charging decisions, and then identifies the recourse structure used by the algorithm in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Two-stage stochastic formulation}
\label{subsec:two-stage-formulation}

Let $\Omega$ be the set of service-plan scenarios, and let $p_\omega$ be the probability of scenario $\omega \in \Omega$, where $p_\omega>0$ and $\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega=1$.
Each scenario represents one plausible future service plan and fixes the route frequencies, timetables, bus trip chains, active-fleet requirements, and trip-level energy requirements, while the operated route set $\mathcal{R}$ is common across scenarios.
The planner first installs long-lived wireless charging facilities before the future service plan is known.
After a scenario is realized, the second-stage recourse selects route-level battery capacities and determines the corresponding representative-day charging allocation and energy management.

Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the station set, $\mathcal{L}$ the directed link set between consecutive stations, and $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}$ the set of existing interchanges or hubs equipped with shared plug-in chargers.
Stationary wireless charging (SWC) can be installed at any station in $\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C}$, while dynamic wireless charging (DWC) can be installed on any directed link in $\mathcal{L}$.
The first-stage binary variables $w_s^{\mathrm{S}}$ and $w_l^{\mathrm{D}}$ indicate whether SWC is installed at station $s\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C}$ and DWC is installed on link $l\in\mathcal{L}$, respectively.
Stations in $\mathcal{C}$ have fixed, limited plug-in capacity and are not candidates for SWC deployment.

The compact two-stage stochastic formulation is
\begin{align}
    \min_{w^{\mathrm{S}},w^{\mathrm{D}}} \quad
        & C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)
        +
        \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} p_\omega \Phi_\omega(w)
        \label{eq:two-stage-model} \\
    \mathrm{s.t.} \quad
        & w_s^{\mathrm{S}} \in \{0,1\},
        && s \in \mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C},
        \label{eq:swc-domain} \\
        & w_l^{\mathrm{D}} \in \{0,1\},
        && l \in \mathcal{L}.
        \label{eq:dwc-domain}
\end{align}
where $w=(w^{\mathrm{S}},w^{\mathrm{D}})$ and
\[
    C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)=\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{C}} c_s^{\mathrm{S}} w_s^{\mathrm{S}}+\sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}} c_l^{\mathrm{D}} w_l^{\mathrm{D}}.
\]
Here $c_s^{\mathrm{S}}$ and $c_l^{\mathrm{D}}$ denote the equivalent daily infrastructure costs, obtained by annualizing the installation costs over the planning horizon and converting them to a representative-day basis.
Battery investment costs are converted on the same basis, so that all objective terms are expressed on a common daily scale.
The vector $w$ is the nonanticipative here-and-now facility decision, and $\Phi_\omega(w)$ is the optimal second-stage value in scenario $\omega$.

Let $z_\omega$ collect the route-level battery selections, $x_\omega$ the plug-in charger occupancy decisions, and $y_\omega$ the charging amounts and battery energy states.
For a fixed facility layout $w$, the scenario recourse value $\Phi_\omega(w)$ is the minimum scenario cost
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_\omega(w)
    =
    \min_{z_\omega,x_\omega,y_\omega}
    \left\{
        C^{\mathrm{bat}}_\omega(z_\omega) + C^{\mathrm{chg}}_\omega(y_\omega):
        (z_\omega,x_\omega,y_\omega)\in\mathcal{P}_\omega(w)
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:recourse-value}
\end{equation}
Fleet-size adjustment costs and the residual value of the existing batteries are scenario constants and are therefore omitted from $\Phi_\omega(w)$.
The next subsection expands $\mathcal{P}_\omega(w)$.

\subsection{Scenario recourse formulation}
\label{subsec:scenario-recourse-formulation}

For a fixed scenario $\omega$ and route $r\in\mathcal R$, let
$\mathcal M_{\omega,r}$ contain the bus trip chains on route $r$.
Each chain corresponds to one bus duty consisting of an ordered
sequence of trips, so
$n_{\omega,r}=|\mathcal M_{\omega,r}|$ is the route fleet
requirement in scenario $\omega$.
The sets $\mathcal V_{\omega,r}$ and $\mathcal A_{\omega,r}$
collect, respectively, all station-visit and directed-traversal
event copies generated by these trip chains.
Each event copy uniquely identifies its trip chain, trip, and
position within the duty, so explicit event-ownership indices are
suppressed for notational simplicity.
Distinct operational occurrences remain separate even when they
correspond to the same physical station or directed link.

Figure~\ref{fig:daily-duty-operation} illustrates the event-level representation of a representative bus duty and the charging opportunities embedded in its sequence of trips.

\paragraph{Battery configuration and cost.}
Battery option $k\in\mathcal K$ has capacity $\beta_k$, and the scenario-dependent binary variable $z_{\omega,r,k}$ indicates whether it is selected for route $r$.
The route-level battery-choice constraints are
\begin{align}
    & \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} z_{\omega,r,k} = 1,
    && r \in \mathcal{R},
    \label{eq:one-battery} \\
    & B_{\omega,r}
    =
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \beta_k z_{\omega,r,k},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},
    \label{eq:battery-size} \\
    & z_{\omega,r,k} \in \{0,1\},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ k \in \mathcal{K}.
    \label{eq:z-domain}
\end{align}
Constraints~\eqref{eq:one-battery}--\eqref{eq:z-domain} select one common battery option for all buses assigned to each route and define the induced route battery capacity $B_{\omega,r}$.
With representative-day equivalent capacity cost $c^{\mathrm{bat}}$, the scenario battery cost is
\[
    C^{\mathrm{bat}}_\omega(z_\omega)
    =
    c^{\mathrm{bat}}
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    n_{\omega,r}
    \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}}
    \beta_k z_{\omega,r,k}.
\]

\paragraph{Shared plug-in charging.}
Plug-in charger use is modeled over scenario time slots $\mathcal{T}_\omega$.
For route $r$, $\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{\omega,r}$ contains the visit event copies eligible for plug-in charging.
An eligible visit event $v$ can use plug-in charging only during $\mathcal{T}_v\subseteq\mathcal{T}_\omega$, and $s(v)$ maps it to its physical station.
Let $q_v^{\mathrm{P}}$ denote the grid-side plug-in energy supplied during visit $v$.
The binary variable $x_{v\tau}$ records whether visit $v$ occupies a plug-in charger in time slot $\tau$; each occupied slot delivers up to $\kappa$ units of grid-side energy, and station $s$ has capacity $U_s$.
The plug-in charging constraints are
\begin{align}
    & 0
    \leq
    q_v^{\mathrm{P}}
    \leq
    \kappa
    \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_v} x_{v\tau},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:plugin-charge-limit} \\
    & \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{\substack{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}:\\ s(v)=s,\ \tau\in\mathcal{T}_v}}
    x_{v\tau}
    \leq
    U_s,
    && s \in \mathcal{C},\ \tau \in \mathcal{T}_\omega,
    \label{eq:plugin-capacity} \\
    & x_{v\tau} \in \{0,1\},
    && r \in \mathcal{R},\ v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r},\ \tau \in \mathcal{T}_v.
    \label{eq:x-domain}
\end{align}
Constraints~\eqref{eq:plugin-charge-limit}--\eqref{eq:x-domain} limit energy delivery during each visit and enforce available plug-in capacity at every station--time pair.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:plugin-capacity} provides the main cross-route coupling within a scenario.

\paragraph{Route-local operations and charging cost.}
Let $y_\omega$ collect the nonnegative continuous charging and energy-state variables $q_v^{\mathrm{P}}$, $q_v^{\mathrm{S}}$, $q_a^{\mathrm{D}}$, $q_m^{\mathrm{N}}$, $e_v^{\mathrm{arr}}$, and $e_v^{\mathrm{dep}}$.
For each traversal event $a$, $\ell(a)$ denotes its physical directed link, while $\mathrm{tail}(a)$ and $\mathrm{head}(a)$ denote its departure and arrival visit event copies.
At daytime and overnight electricity prices $c^{\mathrm{day}}\geq 0$ and $c^{\mathrm{night}}\geq 0$, the scenario charging cost is
\[
    C^{\mathrm{chg}}_\omega(y_\omega)
    =
    c^{\mathrm{day}}
    \left(
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r}} q_v^{\mathrm{P}}
    +
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega,r}} q_v^{\mathrm{S}}
    +
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}_{\omega,r}} q_a^{\mathrm{D}}
    \right)
    +
    c^{\mathrm{night}}
    \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
    \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}}
    q_m^{\mathrm{N}}.
\]
The parameters $d_a$, $d_m^0$, and $d_m^N$ represent traversal and depot-boundary energy requirements, while $\underline{\rho}$ and $\overline{\rho}$ are the admissible state-of-charge fractions.
Trip-level energy requirements are exogenous and independent of the selected battery capacity; each trip chain starts at the upper state-of-charge limit, and overnight depot charging is sufficient to restore every bus before the next representative day.
The route-local operational constraints are
\begin{align}
    & 0
    \leq
    q_v^{\mathrm{S}}
    \leq
    \bar{q}^{\mathrm{S}}_v w_{s(v)}^{\mathrm{S}},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:swc-charge-limit} \\
    & 0
    \leq
    q_a^{\mathrm{D}}
    \leq
    \bar{q}^{\mathrm{D}}_a w_{\ell(a)}^{\mathrm{D}},
    && a \in \mathcal{A}_{\omega,r}.
    \label{eq:dwc-charge-limit} \\
    & \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    \leq
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    \leq
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:soc-arr-bound} \\
    & \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    \leq
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    \leq
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:soc-dep-bound} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    =
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{P}}
    q_v^{\mathrm{P}},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:station-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_v
    =
    e^{\mathrm{arr}}_v
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{S}}
    q_v^{\mathrm{S}},
    && v \in \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:swc-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{arr}}_{\mathrm{head}(a)}
    =
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{\mathrm{tail}(a)}
    -
    d_a
    +
    \eta^{\mathrm{D}}
    q_a^{\mathrm{D}},
    && a \in \mathcal{A}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:link-energy-balance} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{arr}}_{v_m^{0}}
    =
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    -
    d_m^{0},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:initial-boundary-energy} \\
    & e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{v_m^{N}}
    -
    d_m^{N}
    \geq
    \underline{\rho} B_{\omega,r},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r},
    \label{eq:return-boundary-energy} \\
    & \eta^{\mathrm{P}} q_m^{\mathrm{N}}
    =
    \overline{\rho} B_{\omega,r}
    -
    e^{\mathrm{dep}}_{v_m^{N}}
    +
    d_m^{N},
    && m \in \mathcal{M}_{\omega,r}.
    \label{eq:overnight-charge-balance}
\end{align}
Constraints~\eqref{eq:swc-charge-limit}--\eqref{eq:dwc-charge-limit} link wireless charging quantities to installed facilities, while constraints~\eqref{eq:soc-arr-bound}--\eqref{eq:soc-dep-bound} bound the energy states relative to $B_{\omega,r}$.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:station-energy-balance}--\eqref{eq:link-energy-balance} propagate energy through charging and traversals, and constraints~\eqref{eq:initial-boundary-energy}--\eqref{eq:overnight-charge-balance} close each trip chain.

\subsection{Recourse structure}
\label{subsec:recourse-structure}

For a fixed facility layout $w$, the expected recourse term is separable across scenarios.
This yields a standard integer L-shaped reference in which each scenario is evaluated as a monolithic mixed-integer recourse problem over $(z_\omega,x_\omega,y_\omega)$; we refer to this direct decomposition as Natural ILS.

Within each scenario, battery and charging costs are additive across routes, and all constraints except constraint~\eqref{eq:plugin-capacity} involve variables associated with a single route.
The latter aggregates plug-in occupancy across routes at each station--time pair.
That is, the scenario recourse is conditionally route-separable apart from this localized shared-capacity coupling.
The formulation also induces a facility--battery resource order.
Because trip-level energy consumption is independent of battery capacity, retaining all installed wireless facilities while adding facilities and/or weakly increasing route battery capacities preserves operational feasibility.
Moreover, the minimum charging cost under such a resource-dominating configuration is no greater than that under the original configuration.

The recourse structure therefore combines scenario separability, a route-local block structure with limited shared-capacity coupling, and a facility--battery resource order.
Section~\ref{sec:method} makes the scenario-dependent battery configuration explicit through an equivalent lifted value-function reformulation.
The resulting representation, conditional on $(w,z_\omega)$, supports both route-level lower bounding and resource-monotonicity strengthening.
