\section{Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section examines the planning implications and computational performance of the proposed method on instances derived from an operating bus network.
The study first defines a representative base instance, then characterizes one planning solution, evaluates the effect of shared plug-in capacity on the Phase~1 relaxation, and finally assesses the algorithmic components and scalability of the method.

\subsection{Experimental setup and base instance}
\label{subsec:experimental-setup}
The case study is based on the Tower Transit bus network in Singapore.
The network contains multiple interacting routes and shared charging resources, reflecting the cross-route coupling considered in the model.
In the base instance, we select 28 routes to construct the study network displayed in Figure~\ref{fig:case-study-map}.
The resulting network contains 493 stations and 625 directed links, including five existing plug-in charging locations at bus interchanges or hubs.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{figures/study_case.png}
    \caption{Spatial distribution of the Tower Transit bus network used in the case study.}
    \label{fig:case-study-map}
\end{figure}

The base service plan is constructed from official Tower Transit route and timetable data.
In the base service plan, 282 buses depart from two depots and complete 2,850 scheduled trips.
The two perturbed scenarios apply 10\% lower and 10\% higher systemwide route frequencies, respectively, and reconstruct the associated trip chains and route fleet requirements.
The resulting base instance statistics are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:base-instance-statistics}.
These scenarios represent service-plan uncertainty on a fixed physical network; the route fleet requirements and trip chains are scenario dependent.
The three scenarios are assigned equal probabilities.
The three-scenario instance is used as the base case for detailed planning analysis.

\subsection{Algorithmic evaluation}
\label{subsec:algorithmic-evaluation}

An instance is denoted by $N$-$k$, where $N\in\{\mathrm{S},\mathrm{M},\mathrm{B},\mathrm{L}\}$ identifies the small, medium, base, or large network and $k$ is the number of service-plan scenarios.
