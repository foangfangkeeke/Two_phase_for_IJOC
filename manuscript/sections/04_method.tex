\section{A Two-Phase Integer L-Shaped Method}
\label{sec:method}

The two-stage structure of \eqref{eq:two-stage-model} naturally lends itself to an integer L-shaped decomposition \citep{laporte_integer_1993}.
The master determines the scenario-independent facility layout $w$, and each scenario subproblem selects batteries and charging operations under that layout.
We refer to this natural decomposition as ILS.
With $\Phi_\omega(w)=\min\{C_\omega(u_\omega):u_\omega\in\mathcal U_\omega(w)\}$ denoting the scenario value, the exact epigraph formulation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \min_{w,\theta^{\mathrm N}}\quad
 &C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)+\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega\theta_\omega^{\mathrm N}\\
 \mathrm{s.t.}\quad
 &w\in\mathcal W,\\
 &\theta_\omega^{\mathrm N}\ge\Phi_\omega(w),\qquad\omega\in\Omega.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:natural-ils-value-master}
\end{equation}
Here $\Phi_\omega(w)=+\infty$ if the scenario subproblem is infeasible, and $\theta_\omega^{\mathrm N}$ represents its combined battery and charging cost.

\subsection{Variable lifting and valid inequalities}
\label{subsec:lifted-reformulation}

In ILS, battery capacities and their costs are determined within the scenario subproblems and enter the master through $\Phi_\omega(w)$.
We lift the battery-selection variables $z_\omega$ into the master to represent the trade-off between shared facility investment and scenario-dependent battery capacity explicitly.
The resulting master jointly selects facilities and battery configurations across scenarios, accounting directly for battery costs and selection constraints.

The lifted master is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 V^L=\min_{w,z,\theta}\quad
 &C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)+\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega
 [C_\omega^{\mathrm{bat}}(z_\omega)+\theta_\omega]\\
 \mathrm{s.t.}\quad &w\in\mathcal W,\\
 &\eqref{eq:one-battery}\text{--}\eqref{eq:z-domain},
 \qquad r\in\mathcal R,\ \omega\in\Omega,\\
 &\theta_\omega\ge Q_\omega(w,z_\omega),\qquad\omega\in\Omega.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exact-lifted-formulation}
\end{equation}
Here $z=(z_\omega)_{\omega\in\Omega}$ and $\theta_\omega$ represents scenario charging cost.
For fixed $(w,z_\omega)$, the operational subproblem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 Q_\omega(w,z_\omega)=\min_{x_\omega,y_\omega}\quad
 &C_\omega^{\mathrm{chg}}(y_\omega)\\
 \mathrm{s.t.}\quad
 &\eqref{eq:plugin-charge-limit}\text{--}\eqref{eq:x-domain},\\
 &\eqref{eq:dwc-charge-limit}\text{--}\eqref{eq:overnight-charge-balance},
 &&r\in\mathcal R,\\
 &y_\omega\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:operational-recourse}
\end{equation}
Set $Q_\omega(w,z_\omega)=+\infty$ if the subproblem is infeasible.
Together, these problems retain all variables, costs, and constraints of \eqref{eq:two-stage-model}.
We refer to ILS applied directly to this formulation as Lifted ILS.

\paragraph{Energy-budget valid inequality.}
For each $m\in\mathcal M_{\omega,r}$, let $\mathcal A_m$ contain its traversals, and let $\mathcal V_m^{\mathrm P}$ and $\mathcal A_m^{\mathrm D}$ contain its eligible plug-in visits and DWC traversals, respectively.
Usable battery energy and maximum charging supply must cover total duty consumption, yielding the following energy-budget valid inequality (VI):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &(\overline\rho-\underline\rho)\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\beta_k z_{\omega,r,k}
 +\eta^{\mathrm P}\bar q^{\mathrm P}\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_m^{\mathrm P}}|\mathcal T_v|\\
 &\qquad+\eta^{\mathrm D}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_m^{\mathrm D}}\bar q_a^{\mathrm D}w_{\ell(a)}
 \ge d_m^0+\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_m}d_a+d_m^N,
 \qquad \forall\,\omega\in\Omega,\ r\in\mathcal R,\ m\in\mathcal M_{\omega,r}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:energy-budget-vi}
\end{equation}
We add the inequality for the duty with the largest number of trips in each scenario--route pair to the master in both phases.

\subsection{Two-phase relaxation and verification}
\label{subsec:route-lower-bounding}

As the road network and fleet expand, the operational subproblem \eqref{eq:operational-recourse} grows in both charging decisions and shared-capacity constraints, making it more difficult to solve.
To address this computational burden, we develop TPILS consisting of two phases.
In Phase 1, removing the shared-capacity coupling allows occupancy variables to be eliminated, decomposing each large scenario MILP into small, independent route LPs.
Phase 2 restores the exact scenario MILPs while retaining the lower bounds generated in Phase 1.
Figure~\ref{fig:two-phase-framework} summarizes the two phases.

 \caption{Two-phase solution framework for TPILS.}
 \label{fig:two-phase-framework}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Phase 1: Coupling relaxation and route decomposition.}
For fixed $(w,z_\omega)$, we drop the shared plug-in capacity constraints \eqref{eq:plugin-capacity} and eliminate occupancy variables, obtaining
\begin{equation}
 0\le q_v^{\mathrm P}\le\bar q^{\mathrm P}|\mathcal T_v|,
 \qquad v\in\mathcal V_{\omega,r}^{\mathrm P}.
\label{eq:phase-one-plugin-aggregate-bound}
\end{equation}
After the shared-capacity constraints are removed, any charging amount satisfying this bound can be represented by setting $x_{v\tau}=1$ for every $\tau\in\mathcal T_v$.
The remaining constraints involve one route at a time, and charging cost is additive across routes, $C_\omega^{\mathrm{chg}}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}C_{\omega,r}^{\mathrm{chg}}$.
For each $(\omega,r)$, the resulting route LP is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat Q_{\omega,r}(w,z_{\omega,r})=\min_{y_{\omega,r}\ge0}\quad
 &C_{\omega,r}^{\mathrm{chg}}(y_{\omega,r})\\
 \mathrm{s.t.}\quad
 &\eqref{eq:dwc-charge-limit}\text{--}\eqref{eq:overnight-charge-balance},\\
 &\eqref{eq:phase-one-plugin-aggregate-bound},\\
 &q_v^{\mathrm P}=0,\qquad v\in\mathcal V_{\omega,r}\setminus\mathcal V_{\omega,r}^{\mathrm P}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phase-one-route-value}
\end{equation}
All constraints are restricted to route $r$.
Set $\widehat Q_{\omega,r}(w,z_{\omega,r})=+\infty$ if the route LP is infeasible.

\begin{proposition}[Validity of the route relaxation]
\label{prop:phase-one-lb}
For any facility layout $w\in\mathcal W$, battery selections satisfying \eqref{eq:one-battery}--\eqref{eq:z-domain}, and $\omega\in\Omega$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\widehat Q_{\omega,r}(w,z_{\omega,r})\le Q_\omega(w,z_\omega).
\label{eq:phase-one-lb}
\end{equation}
Infeasibility of any route LP certifies infeasibility of the exact scenario subproblem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $x_{v\tau}\le1$, every exact feasible solution satisfies \eqref{eq:phase-one-plugin-aggregate-bound} and retains all other route constraints.
Its route components are therefore feasible for the route LPs with the same total charging cost, proving the lower bound and, by contraposition, the infeasibility claim.
If the exact subproblem is infeasible, the bound holds by $Q_\omega=+\infty$.
\end{proof}

The Phase 1 restricted master is the following program augmented with all accumulated route feasibility and optimality cuts:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \min_{w,z,\widehat\theta}\quad
 &\left[C^{\mathrm{fac}}(w)+\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega
 C_\omega^{\mathrm{bat}}(z_\omega)\right]
 +\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\widehat\theta_{\omega,r}\\
 \mathrm{s.t.}\quad &w\in\mathcal W,\\
 &\eqref{eq:one-battery}\text{--}\eqref{eq:z-domain}, &&\omega\in\Omega,\ r\in\mathcal R,\\
 &\eqref{eq:energy-budget-vi},\\
 &\widehat\theta_{\omega,r}\ge0, &&\omega\in\Omega,\ r\in\mathcal R.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phase-one-master}
\end{equation}
The bracketed term is the facility and battery cost of $(w,z)$; the remaining term approximates route charging costs through proxies $\widehat\theta_{\omega,r}$ constrained by the accumulated route cuts.
Optimal route LP solutions generate dual optimality cuts; infeasible route LPs generate feasibility cuts, using a Farkas certificate when available \citep{benders_partitioning_1962}.
Each cut involves only its route's battery selection and facilities.

Let $LB^R$ be the best Phase 1 master bound and $UB^R$ the lowest evaluated objective for a design with all route LPs feasible.
Each evaluation adds facility and battery costs to the expected charging cost obtained from the route LPs.
The phase transition occurs when
\begin{equation}
 g^R=\frac{UB^R-LB^R}{\max\{1,|UB^R|\}}\le\varepsilon_1.
\label{eq:phase-switch}
\end{equation}
Set $g^R=+\infty$ until $UB^R$ is finite.
The value $LB^R$ also bounds $V^L$, whereas $UB^R$ bounds only the relaxed problem.

\paragraph{Phase 2: Exact scenario verification.}
Phase 2 extends the Phase 1 master to solve the exact lifted formulation \eqref{eq:exact-lifted-formulation}, carrying forward the lower bound $LB^R$ and route cuts.
In the objective of \eqref{eq:phase-one-master}, the sum of the route cost proxies $\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\widehat\theta_{\omega,r}$ for each scenario is replaced by the scenario cost proxy $\theta_\omega$.
Its restricted master approximates the scenario value constraints $\theta_\omega\ge Q_\omega(w,z_\omega)$ with accumulated feasibility and optimality cuts.
The selected energy-budget inequalities are retained, and the route and scenario cost proxies are linked through
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \theta_\omega&\ge\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\widehat\theta_{\omega,r}, &&\omega\in\Omega,\\
 \widehat\theta_{\omega,r}&\ge0, &&\omega\in\Omega,\ r\in\mathcal R.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phase-two-linking}
\end{equation}
At each Phase 2 master solution $(w^*,z^*,\theta^*)$, we first solve one relaxed scenario subproblem (RSP) for every $\omega\in\Omega$.
Let $Q_\omega^{\mathrm{LP}}(w,z_\omega)$ denote the value of the linear relaxation of \eqref{eq:operational-recourse}, obtained by replacing \eqref{eq:x-domain} with $0\le x_\omega\le1$; set $Q_\omega^{\mathrm{LP}}=+\infty$ if the relaxation is infeasible.
The RSP retains shared plug-in capacity across routes and therefore captures interactions omitted by the route LPs.
Its value satisfies $\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\widehat Q_{\omega,r}\le Q_\omega^{\mathrm{LP}}\le Q_\omega$.
In our computational experience, finite RSP values are typically close to their exact counterparts, yielding strong lower-bounding cuts for the master.
As shown on the right of Figure~\ref{fig:two-phase-framework}, we solve the exact scenario MILPs only if all RSPs are feasible; otherwise, we return to the master.
Both RSPs and exact scenario MILPs provide optimality cuts when solved to optimality and feasibility cuts when infeasible.
RSP cuts use LP dual information when available; exact scenario MILPs use integer L-shaped optimality cuts \citep{laporte_integer_1993} and no-good feasibility cuts.
Once a master design is feasible for every exact scenario subproblem, its evaluated cost provides an upper bound.
TPILS terminates when this upper bound and the restricted-master lower bound satisfy the prescribed relative tolerance.

\subsection{Monotone feasibility cuts and repair enhancement}
\label{subsec:resource-monotonicity}

R-TPILS augments TPILS with the repair-enhanced monotone feasibility cuts developed below.
When the exact subproblem for scenario $\omega$ certifies $(w^*,z_\omega^*)$ as infeasible, TPILS adds the following pointwise no-good feasibility cut \citep{elci_stochastic_2022}:
\begin{equation}
 D_\omega(w,z_\omega;w^*,z_\omega^*)=
 \sum_{l\in\mathcal L^{\mathrm D}:w_l^*=0}w_l
 +\sum_{l\in\mathcal L^{\mathrm D}:w_l^*=1}(1-w_l)
 +\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}z_{\omega,r,k}^*(1-z_{\omega,r,k})\ge1.
\label{eq:pointwise-feasibility-cut}
\end{equation}
On the binary domain, this cut excludes only the evaluated facility and battery configuration.
\paragraph{Monotone feasibility cuts.}
Operational feasibility is monotone with respect to DWC availability and battery capacity.
If $(w^*,z_\omega^*)$ is infeasible, any configuration with no additional facilities and no larger batteries is also infeasible.
A feasible configuration must therefore open an additional DWC facility or increase the battery capacity on at least one route, giving the monotone feasibility cut
\begin{equation}
 I_\omega(w,z_\omega;w^*,z_\omega^*)=
 \sum_{l\in\mathcal L^{\mathrm D}:w_l^*=0}w_l
 +\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K:\beta_k>B_{\omega,r}(z_{\omega,r}^*)}z_{\omega,r,k}\ge1.
\label{eq:monotone-feasibility-cut}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Validity and dominance]
\label{prop:monotone-feasibility-cut}
Cut~\eqref{eq:monotone-feasibility-cut} is valid and weakly dominates \eqref{eq:pointwise-feasibility-cut} at the same anchor.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First, consider a feasible configuration $(w,z_\omega)$ and another configuration $(\bar w,\bar z_\omega)$ with $\bar w_l\ge w_l$ for every $l\in\mathcal L^{\mathrm D}$ and $B_{\omega,r}(\bar z_{\omega,r})\ge B_{\omega,r}(z_{\omega,r})$ for every $r\in\mathcal R$.
Keeping the original charging and occupancy decisions, the DWC charging bounds remain satisfied because $\bar w\ge w$, while the plug-in charging and capacity constraints are unaffected.
For each route, write $B_{\omega,r}=B_{\omega,r}(z_{\omega,r})$, $\bar B_{\omega,r}=B_{\omega,r}(\bar z_{\omega,r})$, and $\Delta_r=\bar B_{\omega,r}-B_{\omega,r}\ge0$; increase every arrival and departure energy state by $\overline\rho\Delta_r$.
The common shift preserves station and traversal energy balances throughout each duty.
For every original state $e$, its original bounds imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 e+\overline\rho\Delta_r
 &\ge \underline\rho B_{\omega,r}+\overline\rho\Delta_r
 \ge \underline\rho\bar B_{\omega,r},\\
 e+\overline\rho\Delta_r
 &\le \overline\rho B_{\omega,r}+\overline\rho\Delta_r
 =\overline\rho\bar B_{\omega,r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second inequality in the first line follows from $\overline\rho\ge\underline\rho$ and $\Delta_r\ge0$.
The initial boundary equality is preserved; return energy increases by $\overline\rho\Delta_r$, at least the increase $\underline\rho\Delta_r$ in its lower bound.
In the overnight balance, the increases in the capacity term and terminal energy cancel, so the original overnight charging remains valid.
The transformed solution is feasible at $(\bar w,\bar z_\omega)$.

Consequently, a configuration with no additional facilities and no larger batteries than $(w^*,z_\omega^*)$ must be infeasible.
On the binary domain, these configurations are exactly those with $I_\omega=0$, proving validity.

By the battery-selection constraint \eqref{eq:one-battery}, the battery terms in $I_\omega$ are no greater than their counterparts in $D_\omega$; the same holds for the facility terms.
Thus $I_\omega\le D_\omega$ on the continuous relaxation, so \eqref{eq:monotone-feasibility-cut} weakly dominates \eqref{eq:pointwise-feasibility-cut}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Repair-enhanced monotone cuts.}
Because the objective minimizes cost, the master may generate infeasible configurations close to previously evaluated anchors.
The resulting monotone cuts may therefore provide only limited additional coverage of the infeasible region.
Starting from $(w^*,z_\omega^*)$, the repair search first upgrades batteries and then adds facilities, seeking a feasible configuration and identifying resource-enlarged configurations that remain infeasible.
The last such infeasible configuration found, denoted by $(w^+,z_\omega^+)$, is used as the anchor of the strengthened cut:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 I_\omega(w,z_\omega;w^+,z_\omega^+)&\ge1,\qquad Q_\omega(w^+,z_\omega^+)=+\infty,\\
 w_l^+&\ge w_l^*, &&l\in\mathcal L^{\mathrm D},\\
 B_{\omega,r}(z_{\omega,r}^+)&\ge B_{\omega,r}(z_{\omega,r}^*), &&r\in\mathcal R.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:repair-monotone-cut}
\end{equation}
This cut can exclude a larger region of infeasible configurations.

Figure~\ref{fig:repair-search-example} illustrates the search for one scenario.
Battery upgrades are listed in ascending order of incremental cost, and unbuilt facilities in ascending order of construction cost.
A prefix of length $h$ contains the $h$ lowest-cost entries in the corresponding list.
The first evaluation upgrades each eligible route's battery by one level (point 1).
Because this candidate is feasible, the search evaluates successively halved prefixes of the battery-upgrade list until it finds the first infeasible candidate (point 2).
Starting from point 2, the facility search applies the same prefix-halving procedure to the facility list and stops when it finds the infeasible anchor at point 4.
The additional shaded region shows the increase in coverage relative to the original cut.
